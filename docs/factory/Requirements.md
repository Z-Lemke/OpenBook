# Requirements Document

Status: Proposed

Based on: [accepted Intake Document](Intake.md) (accepted at `d2f94e054749c919fe94f0cd5e0663afaeeca391`) and [accepted Discovery Document](Discovery.md) (accepted at `50b86de2c174af4e0ace7866b42e96e9d72535bb`)

This proposal supersedes the accepted Requirements Document. It requires independent validation and renewed human acceptance before it becomes a basis for Solution work.

## Product outcome

V1 is a local, single-user, generic adaptive-learning product. For any viable subject, it can guide a learner from a stated goal through researched teaching and practice to evidence-based adaptation. Malay is the product owner's primary and most intensive end-to-end validation use case, not the product model or V1's only capability.

Source governance is agent-driven: agents discover the resources needed for a learner and lesson, and the system determines source quality, trust, and reuse through algorithmic signals. H1 owns product intent, prioritization, and acceptance, but must neither choose nor curate a trusted initial source corpus and is not a source-approval gate.

The product is open source. Public availability applies to the product's releasable code and required contributor-facing materials; it does not make learner records, model inputs or outputs, discovered instructional sources, or other source data public by default.

## Functional outcomes

1. A learner can start or resume a subject journey, state a goal and available time, complete adaptive intake or leveling, and review, give feedback on, and adjust a learner-facing syllabus or learning plan with daily-lesson and read-ahead views.
2. The product dynamically provides subject-appropriate lessons and practice through consistent, purpose-built learner interfaces rather than chat-first interaction. An agent may select stable catalog components, arrange a subject-appropriate page layout, and, when the catalog lacks a good fit, generate a fresh subject-appropriate UI component that renders immediately in the learner experience while conforming to the product's layout and style guides.
3. Before making teaching or resource claims, agents research and discover candidate sources relevant to the learner's goal, level, subject, and planned activity. The system records each candidate's coverage and the role it plays; it distinguishes knowledge/evidence sources from community or practitioner/wisdom sources when that distinction is useful to the learning need.
4. The system qualifies, ranks, selects, and reuses discovered sources algorithmically using available quality, trust, relevance, coverage, provenance, permitted-use, prior-use, and outcome signals. It is not seeded by an H1-curated trusted corpus and does not require an H1 source approval to use a source. V1 may use weak or poor sources; their selection and observed effects must remain visible so later selection can improve.
5. A learner receives level-appropriate teaching, practice, feedback, and resource-backed recommendations, paced and made more or less challenging in response to demonstrated capability and learner feedback. Plans and lessons cite or otherwise expose the resource evidence that supported material claims where useful to the learner or reviewer; when needed evidence is unavailable, the source gap is recorded rather than filled by an unsupported claim.
6. Intake, lesson work, checkpoints, retention or transfer evidence where relevant, and learner check-ins update durable capability evidence. That evidence changes the plan, pacing, difficulty, review work, subsequent teaching style, and source-selection/reuse feedback without excessive testing.
7. Learners can inspect durable progress, capability evidence, feedback, and plan changes; after a lapse, they can resume with an adjusted plan.
8. The product persistently schedules and presents spaced-repetition review for learned facts or vocabulary across relevant subjects and learning contexts; review outcomes inform later work.
9. Learner records durably retain goals, availability, assessment and capability evidence, plan and lesson versions, activity and review outcomes, preferences, feedback, and the source, ranking, selection, reuse, and artifact lineage evidence needed to resume, evaluate, audit, and adapt a journey.
10. Agents may generate or revise plans, teaching, activities, and learner UI with identified source grounding. Each generated UI component is retained or logged with its component specification, source or code and provenance, generation input and run information, layout/style-validation result, learner usage, outcomes, and learner feedback. This evidence makes repeated useful patterns visible for deliberate first-class productization, but does not automatically make a candidate trusted platform behavior.
11. A prospective contributor can obtain the open-source code, follow documented local setup and operation instructions, run the relevant local checks, and contribute through documented contribution and project-governance expectations. The project provides a responsible vulnerability-reporting/security-disclosure route that does not require public disclosure before a fix or coordinated response.

## Constraints and operational needs

- V1 is local and single-user. Hosted rollout, multi-user operation, external integrations, authentication/roles, external sharing, commercial delivery, production on-call, and service-level objectives are out of scope.
- V1 is subject-agnostic and dynamically composes learning plans and experiences; validation subjects illustrate capability and do not define a fixed product boundary.
- H1 must not choose, curate, or approve a trusted initial source corpus. Agents discover sources and the system algorithmically determines qualification, ranking, selection, and reuse. No `Approved Source` trust gate or equivalent human approval gate is permitted for ordinary source use.
- Instructional claims and recommendations must be grounded in discovered resource evidence or explicitly record the corresponding evidence gap. Basic source provenance and legal/permitted-use signals must be retained, but their capture does not create a human approval workflow or guarantee source quality.
- Source discovery, source records, qualification/ranking, selection/reuse, lesson use, learner feedback, and outcome signals must be durable and auditable enough to support later improvement of source selection. The exact algorithm, policy, thresholds, and implementation remain Solution work.
- Generated UI components may render immediately only within an explicit constrained trust boundary. The runtime must enforce approved UI capabilities and must not confer implicit server, tool, secret, network, or product-data privileges. This does not authorize blind persistence or re-execution of arbitrary generated code or tools; generated-component retention/logging, execution, reuse, promotion, auditability, and revocation/rollback must remain governed by the boundary.
- The V1 product code and the contributor-facing materials needed to set it up, operate it locally, run relevant checks, contribute, and understand governance must be publicly available for an open-source release. Contribution and governance expectations must state how changes are proposed, reviewed, and maintained at the product-policy level; detailed repository mechanics are Solution work.
- A software license must be selected and recorded before any public release. The license choice is a human-owned decision and is not invented by this proposal. Before release, third-party dependencies, incorporated source materials, and releasable generated artifacts must have their provenance, applicable terms/notices, and compatibility with the selected license and intended redistribution/use addressed. Material that cannot be released under those conditions must not be published as part of the release.
- Open-source code does not make learner data, personal records, credentials, private configuration, model prompts/inputs/outputs, retrieved or discovered instructional sources, source-evaluation records, or other source data public by default. Such data must be excluded from public release unless separately authorized and its applicable terms, privacy, and permitted-use requirements are met.
- The project must publish a responsible vulnerability-reporting and security-disclosure route. Open sourcing does not authorize generated components, generated artifacts, or discovered tools to execute outside the approved constrained UI trust boundary or to acquire implicit privileges.
- H1 owns product intent, priority, and acceptance decisions. A1 proposes and implements only within accepted decisions; pedagogy, subject quality, security, privacy, UX research, and operations remain specialist-wayfinder roles. These roles do not create an H1 gate for ordinary source discovery or use.

## Accepted uncertainty and deferrals

- Validation subjects beyond intensive Malay and the required additional example, exact modalities, pedagogical/calibration method, lesson-template promotion criteria, technology stack, and local persistence design are for Solution, provided they meet these outcomes and evidence.
- The exact source-discovery method; source-quality, trust, relevance, ranking, selection, reuse, and feedback algorithm; treatment of legal/permitted-use signals; thresholds; records; and improvement policy are Solution work. They must meet the agent-driven, auditable, no-human-corpus/no-approval-gate requirements above, and must not turn poor-source observability into a claim that poor sources are harmless.
- Solution must decide the precise generated-component sandbox or renderer, approved UI-capability model, artifact lifecycle, retention/log format, validation mechanism, audit and revocation/rollback controls, and the detailed stable-versus-generated component boundaries. It must preserve immediate learner rendering within the stated constrained trust boundary and must not expand V1 into arbitrary tool execution or implicit privileged access.
- The release license is intentionally undecided and must receive a human-owned decision before public release. Solution may propose implementation and documentation details for local contributor setup, checks, contribution workflow, governance, security disclosure, release packaging, dependency/license notices, generated-artifact treatment, and data exclusion, but may not select the license or release protected learner, model, or source data by implication.
- Deployment, multi-user access, privacy/retention/consent policy, scale, commercial model, integrations, geographic rollout, budget, and concrete production operations are outside V1. Solution must preserve the growth-path guardrail without expanding V1 scope.
- No artifact is automatically promoted to trusted platform behavior, and no arbitrary dynamic-tool execution is authorized by this document. A later human-owned policy decision is required for promotion or any capability beyond the approved generated-UI trust boundary; this is distinct from and must not become a human approval gate for ordinary source discovery, qualification, selection, or use.

## Human decision requested

It makes source discovery, qualification/ranking, reuse, auditability, and outcome-driven improvement system responsibilities, and records that the product is open source while reserving the license choice for a human decision before public release.
